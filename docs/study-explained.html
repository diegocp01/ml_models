The question in one sentence

Given basics like where, when, victim traits, and weapon description, can a model guess if the incident is a violent crime (1) or non-violent crime (0)?

What data was used

  • Public LAPD / Data.gov “Crime Data from 2020 to Present” (updated daily).
  • Notebook pull captured 963,006 rows (output from the data-load cell).
  • Year counts in that run: 2020 199,744; 2021 209,755; 2022 235,059; 2023 231,914; 2024 86,534 (partial year through August).
  • Average crimes per full-year slice in that output: 192,601.20.

Sources in notebook: title/date markdown; “almost 1 million observations and 28 variables”; fetch output Total rows fetched: 963006; yearly value_counts output.

What the model looked at (features)

For the supervised task, features were:

  • Area name (encoded)
  • Victim age, sex, descent (encoded)
  • Weapon description (encoded)
  • Year, month, day, hour

Target: violent_crime — 1 = violent, 0 = non-violent.

Things the notebook noticed before modeling

  • Timing: average occurrence time ≈ 1:38 PM (mean time code 1338.5); crimes spread across the day (25% by ~9:00 AM, 50% by ~2:20 PM, 75% by ~7:00 PM).
  • Places: “77th” and “Central” areas stand out with notably high crime counts in the charts/discussion.
  • Association check: among tested categoricals, premise description had the strongest chi-squared association with crime type.
  • Class imbalance: far more non-violent than violent cases — that shows up in the model scores below.

What that means in plain English

  • Random Forest won among the classical models the notebook compared — higher overall accuracy and much better at catching violent cases than logistic regression.
  • Logistic regression is okay at “non-violent” but misses most violent cases (recall only 0.20).
  • Imbalance matters: a high overall accuracy can still hide weak violent-crime detection if most rows are non-violent.
  • A small neural net reached ~87.4% validation accuracy, in the same ballpark as Random Forest’s overall score.

Also in the notebook (supporting work)

  • Cleaning / missing-value strategy (drop near-empty extra crime-code columns; impute or mark unknowns for weapon/sex/descent where needed).
  • Unsupervised exploration on 2024-only rows (dimensionality reduction + clustering) for compute-limited exploration.
  • A small interactive “prediction playground” for encoded feature inputs (example: Wilshire / age 30 / male / etc.).

Honest limits

  • This predicts a label on recorded incidents, not a crystal ball for future street events.
  • 2024 counts are lower because the data window stops in August.
  • Violent-class performance is the hard part; overall accuracy alone is not enough.
  • Deep-learning class-wise precision/recall are not written up in the notebook the way Random Forest / Logistic are — so they are not claimed here.
